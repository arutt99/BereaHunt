# Project Instructions: Berea Mystery Date Adventure

This is the spoiler-free requirements brief established through the discovery interview. It records the intended experience and development rules; it does not verify any location or contain the eventual story, route, or solutions.

## 1. Project Overview and Primary Goal

The goal is to design, develop, and deploy one polished, mobile-friendly mystery adventure on GitHub Pages for a date outing for me and my wife. We live in the area and will investigate together using one shared iPhone, launching the game from its Home Screen.

The game will transform real locations in Berea, Ohio, and its immediate border areas into a grounded fictional investigation. Historical places and parks/nature are preferred. We will explore, decode messages, investigate evidence, and solve a connected case.

The experience should feel like a professionally designed outdoor escape room combined with an interactive detective game, rather than a simple scavenger hunt or sightseeing tour.

The audience is specifically the two of us, discussing the same puzzle. Prioritize this private outing over support for other group sizes, multiple adventures, or a general-purpose game platform. A publicly reachable game URL is acceptable.

**Both players must experience the mystery as a surprise, including me despite my involvement in planning.** Protect the plot, solutions, and destinations beyond the chosen starting point throughout development.

**The most important project requirement is accuracy.** Real-world landmarks, physical details, routes, historical facts, inscriptions, and environmental clues must be thoroughly researched and verified before being incorporated into puzzles.

The three main experience priorities are:

1. Challenging deductions that are fair, understandable, and logically defensible.
2. Exploring our local area and making familiar places feel interesting again.
3. A coherent, interesting story whose final revelation makes the discoveries fit together.

The clearest failure would be needing a hint or reveal and then thinking, "That was a stretch." Every solution explanation must demonstrate the reasoning rather than rationalize an arbitrary answer.

## 3. Accuracy and Research Standards

**Treat factual accuracy as a nonnegotiable design priority. Aim for 100% accuracy in all verifiable real-world details.**

Never invent, assume, or extrapolate physical features of actual locations.

For every location considered, research and document:

- Exact location and coordinates
- Public accessibility and relevant restrictions
- Operating hours, if applicable
- Parking and pedestrian access
- Permanent architectural and environmental features
- Historical facts relevant to potential puzzles
- Signs, plaques, inscriptions, monuments, and artwork
- Photographs showing relevant physical details
- Any seasonal conditions that might affect gameplay
- Potential changes, renovations, or temporary closures

### Source verification

Prioritize authoritative sources, including:

1. Official municipal, county, and park websites
2. Historical societies, museums, and archival collections
3. Official business websites
4. Recent photographs and street-level imagery
5. Credible local publications and documented firsthand accounts

Cross-check important claims using independent sources whenever possible.

Record the source URL, date of verification, and supporting evidence for each fact.

Where imagery is involved, record its capture/publication date separately from the date it was reviewed. An undated or old photograph does not establish current conditions. Confirm the exact detail used by the puzzle, its legibility, and the intended public viewpoint; the mere existence of a monument is insufficient.

Never present an assumption as a verified fact.

### Verification classifications

Assign each proposed real-world clue a verification status:

**Verified:** The feature is supported by reliable evidence, its relevant details are confirmed, and its availability from the intended public viewpoint has been established.

**Provisionally Verified:** Reliable evidence supports the feature, but some important detail, visibility condition, or current accessibility remains unconfirmed.

**Unverified:** The feature or necessary details cannot be adequately confirmed.

**Rejected:** The feature is inaccurate, inaccessible, unsafe, excessively fragile, or unsuitable for gameplay.

Only verified clues should be used as essential real-world puzzle dependencies in the final game.

If online research cannot establish current visibility or accessibility, explicitly identify the need for on-site verification. Never label a clue fully verified merely because an older photograph or historical document shows it.

### Verification without an assumed on-site helper

Plan around having no independent on-site verifier. Someone may be available to try the app remotely, but that is unconfirmed and would not verify the physical route.

Maintain the same verification standard even without a helper. For a proposed dependency that cannot meet it:

1. Seek better, appropriately current evidence.
2. Replace the physical dependency or choose a better-supported location.
3. Where suitable, redesign the puzzle around a verified reference document or image supplied in the app, with any unconfirmed physical observation clearly optional. Verify the stop's access separately if players are still directed there.
4. If a critical dependency remains unresolved, report that readiness issue without exposing the clue and do not declare the experience ready for release.

A fallback protects against a day-of change or difficulty locating a detail. It does not turn an unverified essential physical dependency into a verified one.

Treat 100% factual accuracy as the design goal and a requirement for rigorous checking. Do not promise that changing real-world conditions have been guaranteed, claim an on-site visit that did not occur, or describe remote software testing as a physical walkthrough.

Recheck time-sensitive access information close to the eventual play date when feasible. Until that date and a recheck are arranged, state the actual research dates and remaining uncertainty. Do not imply that ongoing monitoring or a future check has already been scheduled.

### No fabricated details

Never invent:

- Inscription wording
- Dates on monuments
- Counts of architectural features
- Building colors
- Names appearing on plaques
- Directions or spatial relationships
- Historical events
- Walking distances
- Operating hours
- Public access permissions

Fictional information may be invented freely for the game's narrative, but it must be clearly distinguished from factual information during development.

Generated or altered artwork may illustrate the fiction. It must not be used as evidence that a real inscription, physical feature, or viewpoint exists. Keep authoritative reference imagery and invented story artifacts distinguishable in development records.

## 4. Real-World Puzzle Design

The game must go beyond sending players from one location to another.

Whenever practical, physical locations should be integral to solving puzzles.

Both styles of outdoor investigation are welcome: briefly finding a physical detail to use in a deeper phone-based puzzle, and spending meaningful time comparing or investigating the surroundings. Keep the individual walk short and avoid making progress depend on prolonged, aimless searching.

Examples of appropriate puzzle mechanics include:

- Comparing a fictional photograph or document against a real landmark
- Finding a verified inscription and using it to decode a message
- Identifying architectural details
- Interpreting symbols found on monuments or public artwork
- Connecting historical facts to fictional evidence
- Using real-world observations to complete a cipher
- Interpreting reliable character statements alongside discoveries made at locations
- Combining evidence gathered at multiple stops
- Reconstructing timelines and deducing relationships from reliable evidence

Favor decoding, codes and ciphers, hidden messages, combining objects or information, logic puzzles, inductive reasoning, and document interpretation. Occasional well-supported riddles are welcome. The Exit series is a reference for enjoyable cooperative discovery and satisfying deductions; do not require the physical materials or destructive interactions of a boxed game.

Do not create puzzles that require players to trespass, disturb property, enter restricted areas, approach unsafe locations, or interact with unsuspecting members of the public.

Do not assume that a business will participate in the game unless explicit arrangements have been made.

Do not require printing, bringing writing materials, hiding or retrieving physical game props, making purchases, or obtaining help from staff or passersby. Provide necessary working aids on the phone when a puzzle would otherwise demand extensive notes or manipulation on paper.

### Puzzle quality standards

Each puzzle should:

1. Have a clearly defined objective.
2. Be solvable using information legitimately available to players.
3. Have a logically defensible solution.
4. Require meaningful observation, reasoning, or deduction.
5. Contribute to the story or investigation.
6. Avoid arbitrary answers and misleading ambiguity.
7. Provide satisfying feedback upon completion.

For each intended solution, document why the player can identify the relevant inputs, determine the operation or relationship, and reach the accepted answer. Preserve discovery and insight while ensuring each step is supported.

Reject puzzles that depend on arbitrary extraction orders, unconstrained word association, unmotivated arithmetic, guessing the author's preferred synonym, or finding a tiny incidental detail with no meaningful signposting. Hidden messages are welcome when their discovery and decoding form a fair puzzle. The final story must not hinge on an obscure detail that players could reasonably miss.

If a plausible alternative solution fits the available evidence, revise the clue or accept the equivalent answer. A reveal must explain why the solution follows and resolve genuine ambiguity rather than simply announce the intended answer.

Avoid repetitive puzzles that simply ask players to count objects, read dates, or enter words.

Use a variety of mechanics and difficulty levels.

Target 5–10 minutes on average for a substantial puzzle, with some easier transitions and appropriately deeper challenges. Players should be able to request useful help at any point and should not need to remain stuck beyond roughly 15 minutes. These are pacing targets, not forced hint delays, countdowns, or completion limits.

Design for both players to discuss the same puzzle on one phone. Do not split essential information between devices or require simultaneous separate tasks.

Automatically surface previously unlocked evidence relevant to the current challenge. Some puzzles may combine discoveries from different stops, but do not demand memorization of tiny details or repeated searches through an archive. The reasoning should provide the challenge.

Limited Googling is permitted. Supply core specialist knowledge and decoding references in the game so its offline gameplay remains complete; external searches should be optional and bounded rather than open-ended research assignments.

Not every puzzle must depend on a physical landmark. Some should involve digital evidence, reliable character statements, document analysis, and logical deduction.

Across the experience, aim for approximately 75% examining evidence and entering answers, with approximately 25% hands-on digital interactions. Use manipulation where it helps a particular puzzle, and provide mobile-friendly controls that do not require fiddly dragging or precise alignment.

## 5. Interconnected Narrative Design

The experience should tell one cohesive story rather than consist of unrelated riddles.

The players are investigators in a present-day, grounded real-world mystery with historical connections. Maintain a serious detective tone with potential conspiratorial elements and occasional restrained eeriness. The explanation should be plausible within the real world; do not resolve the case through supernatural causes.

Murder is an acceptable subject. Gruesome or gory presentation is unnecessary. The date occasion does not require a romantic storyline or relationship-based puzzles.

History is included only where it matters to the investigation. Avoid local-history lessons, sightseeing commentary, or trivia questions whose only purpose is to teach a fact. Both players live locally; the case should make familiar surroundings worth examining again.

Every major discovery should advance the mystery.

Players should gradually uncover:

- New evidence
- Evidence whose connections or significance become clearer over time
- Character motivations
- Hidden relationships
- Important revelations
- Changes in their understanding of previous events

The game should encourage players to form hypotheses, reconsider earlier assumptions, and draw conclusions from accumulated evidence.

Where appropriate, include narrative twists that are logically supported by clues encountered earlier.

An earlier player assumption may be overturned through clearly available evidence. Do not use lying witnesses, unreliable witnesses, or an unreliable narrator as a puzzle mechanism. Keep character testimony concise and reliable; avoid extended statement comparisons that turn the game into a reading exercise.

Deliver story through short passages, concise documents, and purposeful visual evidence. Avoid long exposition, excessive names to remember, repeated rereading, and details retained only in the player's memory.

The final challenge should ideally require players to combine information gathered throughout the adventure.

Bring the relevant unlocked evidence together for the final deduction. Keep the submission brief: a short answer or a small choice among options, followed by a deliberate final-submit action. Do not require a lengthy explanation, multiple written fields, or a detailed evidence-selection form.

Follow the final submission with a concise dramatic reveal. Offer an optional full-case explanation showing how the important discoveries fit together, including the reasoning supporting the central conclusion. Keep the eventual answer and precise ending format out of spoiler-free development updates.

Avoid arbitrary twists that cannot reasonably be anticipated or explained.

The fictional narrative should be written around verified real-world locations and features, not the other way around.

## 6. Interactive Game Application

Develop the experience as a mobile-first web application that can be hosted on GitHub Pages.

The primary supported experience is an iPhone web app added to the Home Screen from Safari. It must work smoothly when launched from that icon, including its standalone display and offline behavior. Also support ordinary Safari use; prioritize the agreed iPhone experience over a broad device matrix.

It should feel like a dedicated adventure game rather than a collection of ordinary web pages.

### Core gameplay loop

The primary interaction should follow this sequence:

1. The player receives a story segment, clue, or riddle.
2. The application reveals the relevant task or destination.
3. The player investigates, observes, and solves the challenge.
4. The player enters an answer into the application.
5. The application validates the answer.
6. A correct answer unlocks new information, evidence, story content, or the next location.
7. Progress is saved automatically.
8. The process continues until the mystery is resolved.

The application must prevent players from accidentally encountering later story revelations before unlocking them through normal gameplay.

The case and all mandatory puzzle content must remain playable offline after an initial complete download. The interface must clearly confirm when the adventure is ready offline; opening the first page alone must not be mistaken for downloading all later evidence.

### Required application features

**Chapter progression**

- Sequentially unlocked chapters
- Clear indication of current progress
- Locked future chapters
- Narrative transitions between stages
- Ability to revisit previously discovered evidence
- Future destinations concealed until their stage unlocks
- Relevant unlocked evidence gathered automatically alongside the current task

**Answer validation**

- Text-entry fields for answers
- Support for reasonable capitalization and punctuation differences
- Accepted alternative spellings or equivalent answers
- Appropriate handling of incorrect answers
- Clear confirmation of correct solutions
- No accidental answer disclosure in error messages
- Unlimited attempts, with no score or time penalty
- Useful feedback for recognizable incorrect interpretations, without revealing unrelated solutions

Use simple answer controls appropriate to the puzzle, including short text or a small selection where suitable. Avoid making exact punctuation or an arbitrary phrasing preference part of the challenge.

**Hint system**

Every substantial puzzle should offer progressive assistance:

- Hint 1: Gentle directional guidance
- Hint 2: More specific assistance
- Hint 3: Strong guidance toward the solution
- Reveal Answer: Complete explanation and ability to continue

Players should never become permanently stuck.

Using hints or revealing an answer must not prevent the game from being completed.

Hints are requested by the players, available without forced delays, and carry no scoring or time penalty. Do not use a countdown or pressure players to keep moving. Revealing an answer must include the full reasoning and a clear way to continue; explanations should remain available afterward.

**Evidence collection**

- Digital case files
- Collected documents
- Discovered photographs
- Concise, reliable witness statements where useful
- Relevant notes and observations
- Ability to review previously unlocked evidence

Keep the current objective and relevant evidence easy to access together. Reading a document should not require navigating a complicated simulated desktop, remembering a hidden file path, or repeatedly reopening unrelated material. Inspectable images must be legible and usable on an iPhone.

**Progress persistence**

Use reliable browser-based persistence compatible with GitHub Pages. Preserve the current chapter, solved puzzles, collected evidence, and requested hints/reveals across reloads, app closure, and ordinary interruptions. Support pausing at any point without a timed consequence.

Include an option to restart the experience, with confirmation to prevent accidental data loss.

Both players will progress on the same phone. Accounts, shared sessions, cross-device synchronization, and manual transfer features are not required.

Provide brief spoiler-free Home Screen installation instructions and encourage starting the actual game from the installed app. Verify storage behavior in Safari and the Home Screen app; do not assume the two contexts share a save automatically. Do not promise recovery after browser data is deliberately cleared or removed by the device.

**Navigation**

- Clear current objectives
- Location instructions when applicable
- Optional external map links
- Return-to-case-file navigation
- Accessible controls and readable text
- Minimal unnecessary scrolling or complicated menus

Include occasional puzzles that ask the players to deduce a destination. After a correct solution or requested reveal, confirm the exact destination and provide a verified navigation link and arrival instructions before travel. Never send players to an unconfirmed interpretation of a place name.

Show only the current destination and already unlocked information. A full route preview or map revealing future stops would spoil the experience. Keep useful written address/access instructions available offline; external maps and optional web searches may require a connection.

**Fallbacks**

Every location-dependent puzzle must have a fallback mechanism.

Provide an easy-to-find "Can't access this clue" option. If a landmark is inaccessible, a physical feature has changed, or the player cannot locate the intended detail, first supply a verified reference image or equivalent evidence so they can still solve the puzzle themselves.

Keep "Reveal Answer" as a separate, explicit choice with the complete explanation and ability to continue. Opening reference evidence must not automatically reveal the answer.

If the stop itself is closed or unsuitable, provide a way to continue without entering it. Do not require location tracking, proof of arrival, or a physical visit to unlock the fallback. Cache all fallback material for offline use.

Fallbacks should preserve narrative continuity.

The player must always be able to finish the game.

## 7. Technical Architecture

The initial deployment target is GitHub Pages.

A public URL is acceptable. No authentication or private hosting is required for this outing. Keep personal information out of the game unless explicitly needed and agreed.

Prefer a lightweight, maintainable, static application using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, or an appropriate static-site framework if its benefits justify the additional complexity.

Avoid unnecessary dependencies.

Do not require paid hosting, API keys, or server infrastructure for core gameplay.

### Architecture principles

- Mobile-first responsive design
- Fast loading
- Modular and maintainable code
- Separation of game content from application logic
- Configurable locations, chapters, clues, answers, and hints
- Reliable state management
- Browser-based persistence
- Accessible UI components
- Clear error handling
- Compatibility with GitHub Pages deployment
- Home Screen installation with appropriate app metadata and icons
- Complete offline availability after a confirmed initial download
- Consistent saved progress and cached content across ordinary app restarts and updates

Maintain enough content/logic separation that future edits or another adventure could reuse the work. Deliver this one adventure first; do not build a content-management system, adventure marketplace, multi-team platform, or general-purpose authoring tool.

Store adventure content in structured data files wherever practical.

Keep correct answers and progression logic separate from presentation code.

Use a web app manifest and service worker, or an equivalent static-hosting-compatible approach, for the Home Screen and offline requirements. Include all required story assets, images, interactive puzzle code, hints, reveals, and reference fallbacks in the offline content. Do not rely on third-party image hosts or live APIs to supply essential evidence during play.

Handle incomplete downloads and failed cache updates clearly. Preserve a complete, compatible adventure version and the player's progress rather than mixing incompatible content or resetting a live game. A public internet connection is needed for the initial download; core play afterward must not require it.

Recognize that GitHub Pages is a static hosting platform. Answers embedded in client-side files cannot be truly secret from someone who inspects the source code. Normal gameplay should conceal them, but do not promise server-grade secrecy.
